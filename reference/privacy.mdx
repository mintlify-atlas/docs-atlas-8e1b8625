TableSage connects directly to third-party AI services to perform transcription and generate session outputs. Understanding what leaves your computer and what stays local helps you make informed decisions about what recordings and content you process through the application.

## What Leaves Your Computer

**Session audio** is uploaded to [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/) for transcription and speaker diarization during **Create Transcript**. Importing a recording prepares it locally; creating or re-creating the transcript is when the audio is uploaded.

**Transcript text** is sent to your configured language-model providers — Anthropic, OpenAI, or Gemini — for multiple processing steps: proposing corrections, extracting glossary entries, finding a new Player's lines, and generating Session outputs (Ledger, Scene Breakdown, Summary, Recap Summary, and Player Introductions).

**Campaign context and preparation notes** are also sent to those providers as needed. This includes Player names, Roles, Glossary entries, campaign metadata, and generated records such as ledgers and scene breakdowns. **Create Previously On** and **Generate Opportunities** send campaign history and the notes you enter about the upcoming Session.

**Anything an AI coding agent reads** is sent to that agent's provider if you use [Advanced Help](/guides/advanced-help). TableSage prepares agents such as Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini CLI to help in your workspace, and they can read its logs, records, transcripts, and generated documents. TableSage asks them to check names, dates, and processing records before reading content, but whatever they do read leaves your computer.

<Note>
  Each of these services — ElevenLabs, Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, and any AI coding agent — is a paid, third-party product with its own terms, pricing, and data-handling policy. Review those policies before sending recordings or transcripts that include your players, and before adding anyone who has not agreed to have their voice or words handled this way.
</Note>

## What Stays Local

**Audio cleaning** (noise removal) and **punctuation restoration** run entirely on your computer using locally installed machine-learning models. These models are downloaded from Hugging Face when you first save Settings, but once installed they operate entirely offline.

**Voice matching** — recognizing which Player is speaking by comparing audio to each Player's voice print — also runs locally. Player voice samples are never uploaded anywhere.

**Your workspace files** — session recordings, transcripts, and generated documents — are stored in your workspace directory. TableSage connects directly to the processing providers; it does not operate a separate server-side service that syncs or stores your workspace data.

## Local Model Downloads

When you save Settings, TableSage downloads any missing noise-removal, voice-embedding, and punctuation models from Hugging Face. These downloads do not send recordings, transcripts, or Player voice samples to Hugging Face. The models are stored locally and do not require internet access after they are installed:

- The noise-removal model is stored in a `checkpoints/` folder inside the workspace directory.
- The voice-embedding model is stored in `~/.cache/tablesage/`.
- The punctuation model is stored in the standard Hugging Face cache (`~/.cache/huggingface/` unless `HF_HOME` is set).

## API Keys

TableSage needs one key per external service you use: ElevenLabs, plus whichever of Anthropic, OpenAI, or Gemini your model settings select.

Keys are stored once per user account, outside every workspace, so the same keys apply to every workspace on your machine. A key can instead be supplied through a shell environment variable (`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `GEMINI_API_KEY`), which overrides the stored value for that service.

TableSage itself never transmits your keys anywhere except in authenticating directly with the corresponding provider's API. For exact key storage locations per platform, see [Where Keys Live](/guides/settings#where-keys-live).

## Files on Disk

Inside your workspace:

```text
.tablesage/
  tablesage.db      your campaigns, sessions, players, and glossaries
  settings.yaml     this workspace's settings
  logs/             application logs
  agent-guide.md    instructions for AI coding agents started in the workspace
campaigns/
  <campaign name>/<session number>/   session audio and generated documents
players/            player voice samples
checkpoints/        the noise-removal model, downloaded into the folder you launch from
AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, GEMINI.md   pointers to the agent guide
```

Outside the workspace, per user account:

- Your `.env` credentials file with stored API keys (see [Where Keys Live](/guides/settings#where-keys-live))
- `~/.cache/tablesage/`, holding the downloaded voice-embedding model
- The standard Hugging Face cache (`~/.cache/huggingface/` unless `HF_HOME` is set), holding the downloaded punctuation model

Deleting a workspace directory or a cache directory removes what it contains, but never your stored keys, which live in the separate per-user credentials file. Deleting a workspace also removes its `checkpoints/` folder, so TableSage downloads the noise-removal model again the next time you save settings in a new workspace.
