Once TableSage is installed and your API keys are configured, this page walks you through your first real processing run from start to finish: creating a campaign and session, attaching a recording, identifying who was at the table, and exporting the finished transcript and summary. Follow these steps in order the first time — each one sets up what the next step needs.

<Note>
  Processing time depends on the length of your recording and the speed of the API services you have configured. A three-hour session typically takes ten to twenty minutes for transcription alone. Plan to start a run and check back rather than watching it in real time.
</Note>

---

<Steps>

  <Step title="Launch TableSage from Your Workspace">
    Open a terminal, navigate to the workspace directory you created during installation, and start the application:

    ```sh
    cd ~/Documents/tablesage
    tablesage-rpg
    ```

    TableSage opens the **Welcome** screen.

    <Tip>
      Always launch TableSage from the same workspace directory. TableSage stores all campaigns, sessions, and voice data in the directory you start it from — launching from a different directory creates a separate, empty workspace.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open Settings, Enter API Keys, and Save">
    From the Welcome screen, press **S** to open **Settings**.

    Fill in your API keys:

    - **ElevenLabs** — required for every session; handles transcription and speaker diarization.
    - **OpenAI**, **Anthropic**, and/or **Gemini** — required for whichever providers your chosen model tiers use.

    Then choose a model for each tier using the dropdowns:

    | Tier | Default | Used for |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **High** | `openai/gpt-6-astra` | Session artifacts, Previously On, Opportunities |
    | **Medium** | `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-5` | Glossary extraction, spellcheck, name corrections |
    | **Low** | `anthropic/claude-haiku-4-5` | Remove Bad Utterances |

    Each dropdown offers presets across Anthropic, OpenAI, and Gemini. If you prefer a different model, choose **Custom…** and type a model ID in `provider/model-name` form — for example, `openai/gpt-4o`.

    When you are done, choose **Continue** — or move focus out of a key field and press **C**. On first save, TableSage tests each configured model and downloads local audio-processing models. This may take a few minutes.

    <Tip>
      You only need keys for the providers your selected models use, plus ElevenLabs. If you want to use a single provider for all three tiers, select models from that provider and leave the other key fields empty.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create a Campaign">
    From the Welcome screen, press **C** to open **Campaigns**, then press **N** for **New Campaign**.

    Fill in the fields:

    - **Name** — something recognisable, such as *Salt Marsh* or *Curse of Strahd*.
    - **Description** — a brief note about the campaign (optional but useful).
    - **Game System** — for example, *D&D 5e* or *OSE*.

    Choose **Create Campaign**. Select the campaign in the list and press **E** or **Enter** to open **Campaign Detail**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a Session and Attach a Recording">
    On the **Sessions** tab of Campaign Detail, press **N** for **New Session**.

    - **Name** — a short title for this session, such as *Arrival at the Harbor*.
    - **Date** — the real calendar date of the session in `YYYY-MM-DD` format, such as `2026-10-01`.

    Choose **Create Session**. TableSage opens **Session Detail**.

    Next, attach your recording file. In Session Detail, use the file picker to locate the audio file for this session. TableSage accepts the audio formats FFmpeg can read, including `.mp3`, `.m4a`, `.wav`, `.ogg`, and `.flac`.

    <Tip>
      Use the highest-quality recording you have. Background noise and low volume make transcription and voice matching harder. If you have a lossless or high-bitrate recording, use that one rather than a compressed copy.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add Attendees and Assign Roles">
    The first session starts with an empty **Attendance** list. Add every person whose speech is in the recording, including the game master.

    Press **Tab** to move focus to the Attendance table, then press **N** for **New Attendee**:

    1. In the **Player** combo box, select an existing person or choose **&lt;New player…&gt;** and type their real-world name. Use the player's full name with normal capitalisation so players who share a first name stay distinct.
    2. Assign a **Role**: press **R** to add a character name, or press **G** to mark the player as the Game Master.
    3. Choose **Save**, then repeat for every attendee.

    For example: Jordan Lee is the Player, and *Brother Hald* is their Role. Morgan Reyes is another Player with the *Game Master* Role. A player can hold more than one Role if they voiced multiple characters.

    The **Samples** column shows how many voice samples each player's voice print was built from. A red **0** means the player has no voice print yet — TableSage will learn their voice from this recording during processing.

    <Tip>
      Double-check that every character name is spelled exactly as you want it to appear in the transcript and summary. TableSage uses these names throughout all generated documents.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Process the Session">
    With all attendees listed, press **P** on Session Detail to begin **Process**.

    TableSage chooses the processing workflow based on your attendees' voice prints:

    - **Any attendee has a red 0 (no voice print)** — follow the [Process a Session with New Players](/guides/process-session-new-players) workflow. TableSage will collect voice samples from the recording and ask you to confirm speaker identities before producing the transcript.
    - **Everyone already has a usable voice print** — follow the [Process a Session with Returning Players](/guides/process-session-returning-players) workflow. TableSage can match speakers automatically and move straight to transcript generation.

    TableSage guides you through whichever path applies with on-screen prompts at each stage.

    <Note>
      Processing a long session can take ten to twenty minutes or more. Transcription runs on ElevenLabs servers, and the language-model steps that follow depend on your tier selections and the length of the transcript. You can leave the application running and return when it finishes.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review the Transcript and Approve Corrections">
    After processing completes, TableSage presents the transcript for review. Read through it and look for:

    - **Speaker misattributions** — lines assigned to the wrong player.
    - **Misspelled names** — character names, place names, or invented terms that the transcription service rendered incorrectly.
    - **Correction proposals** — for new-player sessions, TableSage proposes name corrections it inferred from context; review and accept or reject each one.

    Make any adjustments, then confirm to proceed. Approved corrections are applied to the transcript and carried forward into every document TableSage generates from it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Export Session Outputs">
    From **Session Detail**, press **X** to open the **Export** options. TableSage can export:

    - The corrected **transcript**
    - The **session summary** (scene breakdown, key decisions, open loops, and ending situation)
    - A **Previously On** recap suitable for sharing with players before the next session
    - The updated **campaign Glossary**

    Choose the outputs you want and confirm. TableSage writes the files to your workspace directory in a folder named after the session, ready to share or archive.
  </Step>

</Steps>
