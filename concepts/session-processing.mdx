**Session processing** is the pipeline that turns a raw recording into a transcript, a ledger, summaries, and all the other artifacts a group can use and share. Processing follows a fixed sequence of steps. Some run automatically; others pause and ask you to review the work before anything is built on top of it.

## Before You Begin

Two things must be in place before processing can start:

- **Attendance.** Every Player who was at the table must be added as an attendee. A Session with no attendees cannot be processed, and missing attendees cause speaker identification to skip that person entirely.
- **Roles.** Each attendee needs a Role for this Session — a character name, **Game Master**, or another table function. Processing replaces Player names with these Roles before generating artifacts.

Processing also calls external services. You need an **ElevenLabs** key for transcription and keys for the LLM providers you have configured. These are set in **Settings** (press **S** on the Welcome screen).

## Two Workflows

TableSage recognizes speakers by comparing utterances against each attendee's **voice print** — a numeric reference built from their voice samples. The workflow TableSage uses depends on whether every attendee already has a usable voice print.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Returning Players" icon="rotate">
    Every attendee already has a usable voice print. Processing moves directly
    from transcription to speaker identification — no additional steps are
    needed to establish voices.
  </Card>
  <Card title="New Players" icon="user-plus">
    At least one attendee has no usable voice print yet. Processing includes
    extra steps to establish that person's voice from the recording before
    speaker identification can proceed.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

TableSage determines which workflow to use automatically based on the attendee list. See [Players & Voices](/concepts/players-and-voices) for a full explanation of voice prints and what makes a Player "new."

## Returning Players Workflow

This workflow applies when every attendee already has a usable voice print.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import Audio">
    You supply the Session's recording. TableSage imports it as the Session's
    **input audio**, converting it to 16 kHz mono for transcription and voice
    comparison, and creates a normalized copy for review playback. You can
    optionally run noise cleaning to make speech clearer in noisy recordings;
    the original source file is left unchanged.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create Transcript">
    ElevenLabs transcribes the recording and groups speech by speaker
    (diarization). Filler words, false starts, and stutters are omitted, so the
    transcript is a clean record rather than a verbatim one. TableSage then adds
    punctuation to produce the raw machine transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove Bad Utterances">
    TableSage filters out **backchannels** — brief listener acknowledgments such
    as *mm-hmm* that carry no independent meaning. It finds short candidate
    phrases, then asks an LLM whether the preceding line was a question.
    Candidates that follow a question are kept; others are removed. The original
    transcript remains available for comparison.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Speakers">
    TableSage compares the voice in each utterance against the voice prints of
    the Session's attendees. Confident matches receive a Player's name; uncertain
    matches are left as **Unassigned Speaker** for you to resolve during
    transcript review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Extract Glossary Terms">
    An LLM reviews the transcript and proposes names and terms that appear to be
    new campaign vocabulary — places, factions, artifacts, and similar items.
    **You review the proposals** and decide which to add to the Campaign
    Glossary. Accepted terms immediately become available for the spellcheck step
    that follows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spellcheck Against Glossary">
    An LLM compares the transcript with the Campaign Glossary and attendees'
    Player names, then proposes spelling corrections. **You review each
    suggestion** before it is applied. Because the previous step has already
    added new terms, they can be used to correct other occurrences in the same
    Session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Transcript">
    Human review establishes the **reviewed transcript** — the accepted record
    of what was said and who said it. You resolve unassigned speakers, correct
    wrong attributions and text, and remove speech that does not belong in the
    record. The recording remains available as evidence. This is the last quality
    check before generation; errors left here flow into the ledger, summaries,
    and any voice samples later taken from the Session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign Roles to Players">
    TableSage creates the **role transcript**, replacing Player names with their
    Session Roles — *Thorgrim* rather than *Bob Martinez*, or *Game Master*
    rather than *Alice Chen*. Any utterances still marked **Unassigned Speaker**
    after review are dropped at this point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate Artifacts">
    An LLM builds the Session's outputs through a sequence of dependencies:
    transcript sections are identified first (opening recap, character
    introductions, actual play); then the **ledger** and scene breakdown are
    compiled; then **player introductions** are collected; then the **recap
    summary** is distilled; and finally the **session summary** is assembled
    from the ledger with the prior Session's recap prepended.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Improve Player Voice Prints">
    Suitable clips from the reviewed transcript can be added to attendees' voice
    sample collections, refreshing their voice prints for future Sessions.
    **TableSage always asks** whether you want to apply this step. Only a
    carefully reviewed transcript should contribute samples — a mislabeled clip
    teaches TableSage the wrong voice.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  For full guides on running this workflow, see
  [Process a Session with Returning Players](/guides/process-session-returning-players).
</Tip>

## New Players Workflow

This workflow applies when at least one attendee has no usable voice print. All steps from the returning-players workflow still apply. The difference is an additional block of four steps — steps 4–7 below — inserted between **Remove Bad Utterances** and **Identify Speakers** to establish each new Player's voice from the recording.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Import Audio">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. TableSage imports and optionally
    cleans the recording, producing the input audio and a normalized copy for
    playback.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create Transcript">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. ElevenLabs transcribes the recording
    and groups speech by anonymous speaker labels.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove Bad Utterances">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. Likely backchannels are filtered out
    to reduce clutter before identity work begins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Name Corrections (New Players)">
    An LLM compares the transcript with attendees' Player and character names and
    proposes corrections for mishearings — for example, *Thor grim* corrected to
    *Thorgrim*. **You review which corrections to apply.**

    This step matters because corrected names become identity clues for the next
    stage. An introduction like *I'm Jordan, playing Brother Hald* or an
    exchange addressed to *Hald* can point to who spoke, but only if the name
    was heard correctly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Isolate New Speakers (New Players)">
    An LLM reads the Session and proposes utterances that belong to each new
    Player, citing conversational evidence such as introductions or lines
    addressed to that Player. It also identifies anonymous speaker labels
    associated with each new Player.

    Proposals that are not contested by competing Players and that contain enough
    speech are retained as candidate samples. When proposals contain too little
    speech, TableSage can supplement them from the associated anonymous speaker
    group and filter additions for acoustic consistency.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review New Speaker Assignments (New Players)">
    **You review** whether each candidate utterance truly contains the intended
    Player's voice. Listening to the recording is important here: a line like
    *Hald, do the Deep Kin have a name…* mentions a character but may have been
    spoken by a different Player entirely.

    A few certain lines are more valuable than a larger collection of mixed
    voices. When more evidence is needed, TableSage can search for speech
    acoustically similar to the confirmed candidates and surface those additional
    clips for review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Seed Player Voice Samples (New Players)">
    TableSage extracts the confirmed utterances into each new Player's voice
    sample collection and computes their initial voice prints. Clips below a
    minimum length and duplicate or outlier samples are excluded.

    After this step, a new Player with enough usable samples can be recognized
    in the rest of this Session and in future Sessions. Players with insufficient
    evidence still require manual speaker assignment during transcript review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify Speakers">
    Same as the returning-players workflow, now using the newly seeded voice
    prints for new Players alongside the existing voice prints for returning
    attendees.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Extract Glossary Terms">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. An LLM proposes new campaign
    vocabulary for your review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spellcheck Against Glossary">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. Proposed corrections are reviewed
    before being applied.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Transcript">
    Same as the returning-players workflow, with extra attention recommended for
    new Players. Their voices were learned from a small set of samples, so
    speaker assignments for those Players deserve careful checking before you
    accept the reviewed transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign Roles to Players">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. Player names are replaced with
    Session Roles in the role transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate Artifacts">
    Same as the returning-players workflow. The ledger, recap summary, and
    session summary are built from the reviewed role transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Improve Player Voice Prints">
    Same as the returning-players workflow — and especially valuable for new
    Players. A reviewed transcript can dramatically expand the number of voice
    samples for someone who started the Session with none, greatly improving
    their recognition in future Sessions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

  For full guides on running this workflow, see
  [Process a Session with New Players](/guides/process-session-new-players).
</Tip>

## Restarting a Step

Processing steps are checkpointed. If you need to revisit an earlier step — for example, to accept different glossary terms or correct a speaker assignment — you can restart that step and any dependent steps that follow it. Reprocessing the affected stages restores a consistent record across all artifacts.

<Warning>
  Changing an accepted correction or speaker assignment at an earlier step makes
  the transcripts and artifacts generated after that point stale. Always
  reprocess the dependent stages to keep the record consistent.
</Warning>
